# The Composable Social Interaction Manifesto

*A science-grounded philosophy for programmable human connection.*

---

## Preamble

Social life is the single highest-leverage domain in the human experience, and it is the last one we have refused to engineer. We engineer our calendars, our workouts, our diets, our finances, our code, our sleep — but when it comes to the way we meet, befriend, court, repair, and grow with other humans, we still operate on folklore, fragments of advice, and whatever scripts we happened to absorb before age fifteen.

This manifesto proposes a different way. We believe that **human social behavior is composed of discrete, learnable, composable units** — and that treating those units as first-class programmable objects is not cold, not manipulative, and not dehumanizing. It is the opposite: it is the most honest, most effective, and most *liberating* thing a person can do for their own social life.

This is a system for people who want to get better on purpose.

## Part I — The Science

The theory is not new. We are stitching together six converging lines of research that, taken together, already describe how social behavior actually works. The novelty is treating these findings seriously enough to build tools around them.

### 1. Script Theory (Schank & Abelson, 1977)

In the foundational work *Scripts, Plans, Goals, and Understanding*, Schank and Abelson showed that human social cognition runs on **stored event sequences** — mental templates like "ordering at a restaurant" or "greeting a colleague." These scripts are not metaphors. They are the literal cognitive scaffolding by which humans navigate predictable social situations with minimal conscious effort.

The implication is radical: **social fluency is script fluency.** People who seem "naturally good with others" are not channeling mystical charisma — they are running a deeper, richer, better-indexed library of scripts, often assembled unconsciously over decades.

If scripts are the substrate, then scripts can be authored, shared, refined, and composed. That is what this system does.

### 2. Chunking and Procedural Memory (Miller 1956; Anderson 1982; Ericsson 1993)

George Miller's "magical number seven" established working memory as painfully finite. Chase and Simon's chess expertise studies (1973) showed masters do not have bigger memories — they have **bigger chunks**. Ericsson's deliberate-practice research extended this across domains: expertise is the progressive compression of raw moves into higher-order, retrievable units.

**A social mini-sequence is a chunk.** "Approach a stranger in a coffee shop" is, for an expert, one smooth action. For a beginner, it is forty-three micro-decisions competing for the same seven working-memory slots. Externalizing the sequence — writing it down, naming it, running it, reviewing it — is the classical path from novice to expert that every other skilled domain already uses openly.

We are simply declaring that **social skill deserves the same treatment as chess, music, surgery, and sport.**

### 3. The Extended Mind (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)

Clark and Chalmers argued that cognitive processes routinely extend beyond the skull — into notebooks, phones, environments, other people. A mind is not where the neurons are; it is wherever the causally relevant loops run. This is now mainstream in philosophy of cognition and embodied neuroscience.

**A library of social sequences is a prosthesis for memory and planning.** It is not a crutch any more than a grocery list is a crutch. It is how a finite brain accomplishes more than its finite working memory should allow.

The person with a curated, personalized, living library of social sequences *is, literally, cognitively enhanced* in the social domain. This is not metaphorical. It is the Clark–Chalmers thesis applied to the one domain where people still feel ashamed to use tools.

### 4. Behavioral Activation and Graded Exposure (Lewinsohn 1974; Foa & Kozak 1986)

Two of the most replicated findings in clinical psychology: depression responds to **structured activation** (small, concrete behaviors, tracked and repeated), and anxiety responds to **graded exposure** (hierarchies of approach behaviors, each slightly harder than the last).

Both interventions work by turning fuzzy, overwhelming life goals ("be more social", "stop being afraid of rejection") into **discrete, named, sequenced units of action** — and then running them. This is literally what our system does. The clinical evidence is already overwhelming that when you atomize a social goal into small sequenced steps, outcomes improve dramatically.

We are not inventing the intervention. We are giving it a runtime.

### 5. Habit Loops and Cue–Routine–Reward (Graybiel 2008; Duhigg 2012; Wood & Rünger 2016)

Habit research, grounded in basal-ganglia neuroscience, shows that durable behavior change requires three things: a **cue** (context trigger), a **routine** (the chunked behavior), and a **reward** (reinforcement feedback). Research on implementation intentions (Gollwitzer 1999) shows that *explicit if–then plans* dramatically outperform vague goals — often by a factor of two or three.

A mini-sequence is an implementation intention with teeth. "If I see a person I'd like to meet, then I run Approach-v3" is exactly the kind of cue-indexed routine the habit literature identifies as the active ingredient of behavior change. Attaching outcome-tracking to each run gives the reward loop honest data to close on.

### 6. Deliberate Practice (Ericsson, Krampe, & Tesch-Römer 1993)

Deliberate practice has four requirements: (1) a specific, well-defined goal, (2) full concentration, (3) immediate feedback, (4) repetition with refinement. It is the only known path to genuine expertise, across every domain it has been studied in.

Social life, as traditionally practiced, violates all four. Goals are vague. Feedback is delayed or absent. Repetition is unstructured. Refinement happens — if at all — through painful intuition.

**Atomized, tracked, composable sequences convert social life into a deliberate-practice environment.** That is the entire theoretical claim of this manifesto, and it is the specific mechanism by which users of this system will improve faster than users of any previous approach, including therapy, coaching, and self-help books. Not because the sequences are magic, but because the practice structure finally matches what the evidence says works.

## Part II — The Philosophy

The science tells us *what* to build. The philosophy tells us *why*, and on whose terms.

### Principle 1: Atomization Over Monoliths

The fundamental move is to break social life into the smallest units that still carry meaning on their own. Not "dating" — that is a universe. Not "first date" — that is still a multi-hour epic. But "suggest a specific place after she expresses mild interest" — that is an atom. Atoms can be named. Atoms can be run. Atoms can be evaluated. Atoms can be swapped.

Monolithic advice ("just be yourself", "be confident") fails because it cannot be practiced. Atomized sequences succeed because they *must* be practiced in order to exist.

### Principle 2: Composition Over Prescription

No sequence knows what should come next. A mini-sequence that succeeds may *suggest* successors — especially if it was published by a practitioner who has seen what typically works — but the **user always chooses**. Success at "approached a stranger" does not automatically launch "ask to dinner." The user might instead attach "discover shared interests", or "casual low-pressure follow-up", or nothing at all.

This matters because real social life is contextual, and no marketplace author knows your life. The system ships *parts*, not *plans*. Plans are assembled at the edge, by the person living them.

### Principle 3: Sovereignty Over Scripts

Nothing in the system can run without the user's consent, and every sequence is forkable, editable, and deletable. Marketplace sequences are **suggestions**, never obligations. The user owns their library, their history, their attachments, and the state attached to every contact. There is no algorithmic nudge, no engagement metric, no behavioral dark pattern. The system exists to serve the user's deliberate intent, not to harvest their attention.

This is the ethical line that separates a tool from a manipulator.

### Principle 4: Per-Contact State, Not Global Averages

Every human relationship is its own trajectory. The system therefore attaches sequence state **to the contact, not the user.** The history of what you've run with Alice is separate from the history of what you've run with Ben, because Alice and Ben are different people with different contexts, rhythms, and stakes. The library is global; the runs are local to the relationship.

This is the opposite of how most social apps work, and it is why most social apps quietly make people worse at relationships over time.

### Principle 5: Honest Feedback Loops

Every run ends with an outcome tag. Not a score — a tag. *Went well. Went badly. Unclear. Felt authentic. Felt forced. She laughed. He pulled away.* Over time, the aggregate of those tags becomes the user's ground truth about which sequences work for them, in which contexts, with which types of people. This is deliberate practice requirement #3 — immediate, honest feedback — finally installed in the social domain.

The feedback is private, user-owned, and never sold. It exists so the user can get better. Nothing else.

### Principle 6: Marketplace as Ecosystem, Not Authority

Sequences published to the marketplace are contributions to a commons — graded, rated, forked, remixed. No one authority dictates the "right" way to approach a stranger or recover from a conflict. Different communities will develop different sequence libraries with different values. A person who wants warm, slow, consent-forward courtship will subscribe to one library; a person who wants direct, confident, high-tempo interaction will subscribe to another. Both are legitimate. The system does not pick sides.

What the system *does* enforce is transparency: every sequence shows its outcomes, its forks, its lineage. You can see where a sequence came from, how it has been refined, and what the aggregate results look like before you run it.

### Principle 7: Programmability as Liberation

The deepest claim of this manifesto is that **the ability to program your social life is emancipatory.** For millennia, social skill has been distributed by accident — by family, by neighborhood, by the randomness of who happened to model good behavior in front of you as a child. People born into warm, socially fluent environments absorbed thousands of unspoken scripts. People born into cold, isolated, or traumatic environments absorbed different ones, or none, and then spent their adult lives paying for the deficit.

This is not fair, and it is not fixed. Composable, shareable, practicable social sequences are the first technology in human history that can systematically redistribute social fluency to anyone willing to practice. It is the literacy moment for interpersonal skill.

## Part III — The System

From the science and the philosophy, the architecture follows:

- **Mini-sequences** are the atoms. Each is a named, self-contained unit of social behavior with defined preconditions, steps, and outcome tags.
- **Attachments** bind a sequence run to a specific contact, carrying state forward.
- **Chains** are user-composed sequences of mini-sequences, assembled at runtime from any source — personal library, community, marketplace.
- **Suggestions** are metadata: a sequence may propose compatible successors, but never requires them.
- **Outcomes** are tagged per run, stored per contact, and aggregated privately for the user's own review.
- **Libraries** are forkable, shareable, and sovereign. Users own everything downstream of a fork.
- **The marketplace** is a public commons of sequences with transparent lineage and aggregate outcomes. Nothing is hidden, nothing is prescribed.

Under the hood, the permission and relationship substrate is provided by Capbit — atomized authorization as data, matching the atomized-behavior philosophy of the upper layer. Every sequence run, every attachment, every shared library is a capability grant that the user can inspect, revoke, and audit. The infrastructure's philosophy and the product's philosophy are the same: **atomize, compose, make it visible, give the user the keys.**

## Part IV — What This Is Not

- **Not a manipulation manual.** Every sequence is visible to the person running it. Nothing is hidden from the user. The system exists to help the user act *more authentically on their own values*, not to trick other people.
- **Not a replacement for warmth.** Sequences are scaffolding, not substitutes. The point of practicing "approach a stranger" a hundred times is that, eventually, you no longer need to think about it — and what remains is the genuine warmth underneath.
- **Not a social credit system.** There is no global score, no ranking, no public profile of who ran what. Outcomes are private. Feedback is internal.
- **Not a dating app.** Dating is one domain; the system is general. Friendship, family repair, professional networking, difficult conversations, reconnection with estranged people, conflict resolution, mentorship — all are domains of composable social sequences.
- **Not a prescription.** There is no "correct" library. The system is a runtime; the content is plural and contested, as it should be.
