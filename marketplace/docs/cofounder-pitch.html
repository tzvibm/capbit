Human expertise for online dating, sold by the message

A marketplace where dating specialists — opener experts, bio doctors, photo reviewers — sell micro-consultations inside a monetized chat. The pitch, the product, the architecture, and why two people can build it: all of it below. I want you to poke holes in this.

From Tzvi Re Founding partnership Date Jul 2026 Read time ~12 min
01

The thesis in three sentences

Online dating help is a proven market that's currently sold in exactly two broken form factors: $1,000–3,000/month concierge services for the rich, and AI reply-generators for everyone else. Nobody sells the thing most people actually want — five minutes of a genuinely skilled human's attention on this specific conversation, for the price of a coffee. We unbundle premium dating expertise into per-message micro-consulting, take a marketplace cut, and position hard against AI: real humans, verifiable track records.

Fiverr proved services unbundle. Cameo proved parasocial micro-transactions. Twitch proved people pay real money inside a chat. Nobody has run that playbook on dating expertise.

02

The market evidence

Willingness to pay is not hypothetical — the premium end already clears at absurd prices:

  • VIDA Select (done-for-you dating management): $995–2,995/month, month-to-month, operating for years.
  • Celebrity ghostwriters like Meredith Golden charged ~$2,000/month to run clients' apps — with a waitlist.
  • Fiverr dating gigs (profile rewrites, ghostwriting) clear at $99–400 despite zero dating-specific UX: no screenshot-native chat, no dating-specific reputation, no metered follow-up.
  • Matchmakers run $20k–300k. The coaching industry overall is a $6–7B market growing steadily.

Meanwhile the demand side is enormous and miserable: hundreds of millions of dating-app users, and the dominant complaints — dead conversations, no replies, bad profiles — are precisely coachable, per-instance problems. The gap between "$5 impulse purchase" and "$1,000/month package" is an empty price band with proven demand on both sides of it.

Why AI makes this stronger, not weaker

The obvious objection: "ChatGPT writes openers for free." Three answers. (1) Positioning: as AI-generated messages flood the apps, they converge on the same voice — detectable and increasingly disliked. "A human who's actually good at this" becomes the premium, differentiated product, the way "handmade" premiumized against mass manufacturing. (2) Accountability: an AI has no win-rate; a coach has a rating, reviews, and a track record on real outcomes. (3) Judgment beats generation: most of the value isn't writing the message — it's a skilled read of the situation: this conversation is salvageable, this one isn't, your photos are the problem, ask her out now. That's taste plus experience, and it's exactly what generic LLM output is worst at.

03

The product

One core loop. A client browses coaches by specialty → opens a chat → drops a screenshot of their conversation → the coach replies, and each reply is metered against the client's prepaid balance → client rates the coach afterward. Reviews compound into reputation; reputation drives discovery; discovery drives coach supply.

The supply-side insight is that coaches don't pick from our product catalog — they compose their own menu. Every offering is three orthogonal choices: what they help with (openers, bio, photos, conversations, date planning, intros, custom), how it's delivered (in chat, as a returned deliverable, live session, or package), and how it's charged:

The custom-quote path is the closure property: any monetization scheme a coach invents that we didn't anticipate degrades gracefully to a quote. We never ship a new charge type again. That's the difference between a marketplace that scales supply-side creativity and one that bottlenecks on our roadmap.

The line we deliberately draw

Coaching, not impersonation. Coaches advise and draft; clients send. Nobody operates a client's dating account. This kills most of the category's ethical/PR risk (the ghostwriter-catfishing problem), keeps us clear of dating-app ToS enforcement, and — usefully — is also the cheaper product to build. The done-for-you tier can be a deliberate later decision, not an accident.

04

The architecture — why this is small

This is the part I most want your eyes on, because it's the argument that two people ship this. The whole system collapses onto five primitives, and four and a half of them are solved problems with battle-tested implementations:

1 · Catalog

Offer

Anything a coach sells. We adopt Stripe's Product/Price semantics wholesale — one-time, recurring, metered, tiered — rather than inventing a pricing model. If it can't be a Stripe Price, it's a quote.

2 · Metering

UsageEvent

{actor, offer, qty, unit, ref} — the one shape every consumption emits: a reply, 3 photos, 30 minutes. Standard usage-billing model (OpenMeter/Lago). Billing never learns about product features.

3 · Money

Multi-asset ledger

Double-entry, append-only. The unifying trick: pack entitlements are just balances in another asset — escrowed USD and maya:message units are the same mechanism. Escrow, packs, fees, refunds, payouts: one ledger. (And a stored-balance wallet, if we ever add one, is just one more account — no redesign.)

4 · Lifecycle

Order

One state machine (REQUESTED → [QUOTED] → FUNDED → DELIVERED → RELEASED, with dispute/refund branches) wears every costume: deliverable, quote, booking, intro.

5 · Surface

Thread

Chat is a dumb ordered message stream. Monetization is one guard on the coach's send path: active engagement with units or escrow left? → emit UsageEvent → post ledger txn → deliver. Chat knows nothing about money.

Roughly 80% assembled from popular, proven components; the 20% we write is exactly the part that couldn't be anything else. Notably we don't adopt Rocket.Chat/Matrix — their bulk is features we don't need, and the one thing we do need (a billing interceptor inside message-send) is what they make hard. A messages table plus Supabase Realtime is less code than integrating either.

The one invariant that keeps money safe

Every value movement — top-up, charge-on-reply, escrow release, refund, payout — is a server-side double-entry transaction in one code path. Clients never mutate balances; balances are always derivable by folding the ledger. Get this boundary right on day one and everything else can move fast without ever risking someone's money — and every support dispute is answerable from the ledger.

05

Business mechanics

Plain dollars, charged per item at purchase (saved card / Apple Pay — one tap after the first buy), 20% take rate — under Fiverr's effective ~25%+, above payment-only rails. No credit currency: customers see prices they recognize and statements they trust, which matters enormously for first-purchase conversion in a trust-sensitive category. The microtransaction-fee problem is handled with micro-charge batching (several $4 replies → one daily charge) and by packs, which are just items that include N units. A stored-balance wallet stays in our back pocket as a later optimization for heavy users — float and lock-in upside without making newcomers learn a currency on day one.

Cold start — the honest plan

Marketplaces die on liquidity, so we don't launch a general marketplace. We launch one niche — men on Hinge who need opener/conversation help is the likely wedge (highest pain, most willing to pay, easiest to reach) — with 10–20 hand-recruited coaches from the places this expertise already lives unmonetized or badly monetized: dating-advice Reddit, TikTok/IG dating-coach creators, top Fiverr sellers wanting better tooling and metered follow-ups. Supply-first works here because each coach has an audience — coaches bring their own first clients, which is the cheat code most marketplaces don't get.

Disintermediation

The classic leak: client and coach take it off-platform to skip the fee. Defenses are product, not legal: the screenshot-native chat with full history is the workflow; entitlements and reviews are non-portable; payment friction off-platform (Venmo begging) is worse than our 20%; and at $4 a message there's barely anything to disintermediate — the fee saved is cents. High-ticket relationships will leak; the metered core won't.

06

Risks, straight up

Liquidity fails in the wedge nicheExistential

Not enough clients for the first coaches, or vice versa; the loop never spins.

Mitigation — Supply-first with audience-carrying coaches; concierge-match the first hundred engagements by hand; kill criteria defined up front (if repeat-purchase rate is dismal after N weeks, the thesis is wrong and we stop).

Quality variance poisons early trustHigh

One cohort of bad coaches and early clients never return.

Mitigation — Application-gated first cohort, not open signup; reviews only from completed paid engagements; money-back on first purchase (cheap at these price points, buys enormous trust).

Privacy — this data is radioactiveHigh

Clients upload real conversations and photos of third parties who never consented. A leak here isn't a breach disclosure, it's a scandal.

Mitigation — Treat as a feature: aggressive retention limits on uploads, optional face/name blurring, self-serve deletion, never train or market on client content. "The discreet one" is a moat in this category.

AI undercuts the price floorMedium

Free AI gets good enough that $4/reply feels expensive.

Mitigation — We sell judgment and accountability, not text generation (§02). Coaches will use AI as their own tooling — fine; the client pays for the human's taste and track record. If human-verified advice loses to free AI everywhere, the whole coaching economy dies with us — a risk we're comfortable sharing with a $7B industry.

Category ick / platform ToSMedium

Dating-help brushes against ghostwriting's catfishing reputation, and dating apps prohibit account delegation.

Mitigation — The coaching-not-impersonation line (§03) is drawn precisely here: we never touch client accounts, so app ToS aren't implicated and the ethics story is clean — arguably cleaner than the AI tools autogenerating intimacy at scale.

07

Why now, why us, and the ask

Why now: dating-app fatigue is at an all-time high while the apps monetize worse than ever; AI slop is flooding matches and creating the demand for verified human help; the creator economy has normalized experts monetizing micro-attention; and Stripe Connect plus the OSS stack above has made a two-person marketplace genuinely feasible in a way it wasn't five years ago.

Why us: this is an execution-and-liquidity game, not a deep-tech game — it rewards a small team that ships fast and iterates on the loop. The architecture in §04 is deliberately shaped so the two of us cover it: one of us owns the money spine (ledger + Stripe + orders), one owns the surface (thread, composer, discovery), and the seams between those are two narrow interfaces (UsageEvent in, ledger transaction out).

The build: milestone-sequenced to de-risk the scary parts first — money rails before chat, chat before polish. M0 browsable marketplace on seeded data → M1 per-item Stripe purchases + Connect payouts moving real money → M2 the metered thread (the product) → M3 reviews and discovery → M4 safety, seed supply, private beta. Nothing in the plan requires funding to reach a live loop with real transactions; revenue is literally the first feature.

The worst case is we spend a few months building a small, elegant system and learn the demand isn't there. The base case is a niche cash-flowing marketplace. The good case is the Fiverr of a category that doesn't have one yet.

The ask: partner with me on this as technical co-founder. First commitment is deliberately small — pressure-test this memo, then build M0–M2 with me and get the loop live with the first ten coaches. If the loop doesn't spin, we'll know fast and cheap. If it spins, we're the first mover in the price band everyone else skipped.
