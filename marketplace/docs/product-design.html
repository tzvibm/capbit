01

Who it's for

Two sides, and the marketplace only works when both show up. The whole design bends toward making the client's first paid interaction feel low-risk and the coach's earning feel effortless.

Demand side

The stuck dater

Has matches but stalls — dead conversations, a bio that isn't landing, no reply to a great match. Won't commit to a $1,000/mo coach, but will pay $5–20 to get unstuck on this conversation, right now.

Supply side

The specialist coach

Good at one slice of dating and wants to monetize it without building a business. Signs up, sets prices, gets paid per reply. Reputation compounds through ratings and response-time badges.

Why this beats the incumbents

Willingness to pay is already proven at the premium end — ghostwriters charge ~$2,000/mo, done-for-you services like VIDA Select run $995–2,995/mo, and Fiverr profile gigs sell at $99–400. Our wedge is unbundling that into per-message micro-help, reaching the far larger mass market that will never buy a monthly package but will happily pay for one good opener.

02

The core loop

Everything else is scaffolding around this one transaction. If this loop feels good, we have a product.

1

DiscoverClient browses coaches by specialty, price, rating, and response time; opens a profile.

2

Pick an item & pay in dollarsClient chooses off the coach's menu and pays right then via Stripe (saved card / Apple Pay) — a plain one-time charge, no credits or wallet to pre-fund. This opens the engagement with explicit terms.

3

Start the chatClient opens the thread with the coach, drops a screenshot of the match / conversation, asks their question.

4

Coach fulfills — metered by the engagementFree clarifying messages cost nothing; fulfilling replies consume the engagement's terms (per-reply, pack counter, timer). Funds move client → escrow → coach earnings, minus platform fee.

5

RateClient leaves a star rating + review. It aggregates onto the coach's profile and drives discovery.

6

Coach cashes outEarnings accrue to a Stripe Connect balance and pay out on schedule.

03

Coach specialties

A coach picks one or more. Specialties power discovery filters and let people niche down and build a focused reputation — an "opener expert" reads more trustworthy than a generalist.

Opener rescue

Crafting the first message to a specific match so it actually gets a reply.

Bio & profile review

Rewriting prompts and bios to land the right tone and hooks.

Photo feedback

Ordering, culling, and picking the shots that convert (async, screenshot-based).

Conversation keeper

Reviving a stalling chat and steering toward the ask-out.

Date planning

Turning a warm thread into a concrete, good first date.

Full profile makeover

A packaged deliverable across bio + photos + first openers.

04

Data model

The shape that makes metered chat + payouts + reviews work. Postgres, with row-level security so a client only ever reads their own threads and a coach only their own. primary bits shown

profilesone row per user; a user can be client and coach
id · display_name · avatar_url · is_coach · headline · bio · specialties[] · price_per_reply_cents · response_time_mins · rating_avg · rating_count · stripe_account_id
packagesoptional fixed-scope offers a coach sells
id · coach_id → profiles · title · description · price_cents · specialty
threadsone client ↔ one coach conversation
id · client_id → profiles · coach_id → profiles · package_id? · status · created_at · last_message_at
messagesthe metered unit
id · thread_id → threads · sender_id → profiles · body · attachment_url? · amount_charged_cents · is_billable · created_at
money_ledgerappend-only; source of truth for all money movement
id · user_id → profiles · delta_cents · reason (purchase · charge · earning · payout · refund) · ref_id · created_at
reviewsone per completed thread
id · thread_id → threads · client_id · coach_id · stars · body · created_at
Why a ledger, not a balance column

Escrow, pack units remaining, and coach earnings are all derived by summing money_ledger. Money-adjacent state that you can't reconstruct is how you end up with disputes you can't answer — an append-only ledger makes every cent traceable to a purchase, a delivery, or a payout.

06

Money mechanics

Everything is plain dollars, charged per item at the moment of purchase via Stripe (saved card / Apple Pay). No credit currency, no wallet to pre-fund — customers see prices they recognize and card charges they trust. Per-reply items charge the saved card per fulfillment (batched into one daily charge when several land the same day, keeping statements clean and per-transaction fees sane); packs are just items that include N units. A stored-balance wallet is a possible later optimization for heavy users, not the launch mental model. Platform take rate: 20%.

Worked example — a $25 pack purchase

Client buys "10 replies" pack (one card charge)$25.00
Held in escrow, consumed as coach delivers10 replies
Platform take (20%)+$5.00
Coach payout (pre-Stripe fee)$16.00

Flexible messaging: the client sets the terms; the coach fulfills within them

A purchase always starts with the client: they pick an item off the coach's menu (or accept a coach's proposal), which opens an engagement in the thread — a pinned purchase context with explicit terms. The coach's responses then consume the engagement according to its type: per-reply items consume a unit per answer, timed items run a visible timer started by mutual tap, packs decrement a pinned counter ("7 of 10 left"), flat deliverables move through an order card, and custom requests become a coach proposal the client accepts first. Delivery is what completes the purchase. Within an active engagement the coach keeps the flexibility that matters — but it only ever points downward or through consent:

Coach send modeWhat happensUse case
Free messageNo chargeClarifying questions, follow-ups, goodwill
Fulfilling replyConsumes the engagement at its agreed terms, cost badged in threadThe actual advice
Discounted replyCoach waives or under-charges a consumption"Quick one — on the house"
ProposalNew item/price as a card → only charged if client accepts"This needs real strategy — $10, ok?"
Grouped answerSeveral bubbles, one chargeA 3-message answer shouldn't cost 3×

The invariant that makes the system trustworthy: a coach's action can cost the client exactly what they already agreed to — or less. Never more. Trust stays symmetric: every consumption is visibly badged against its engagement with a running total; a low-balance client can still receive free messages (no dead-end threads) but can't open new engagements; "first reply free for new clients" is just an offering default — a trial mechanic with zero new machinery; and client messages — uploads included — are always free, since consumption attaches to the coach's fulfillment, never the client's act of sending.

Design tension to watch

Disintermediation. Once a client trusts a coach, both are tempted to take it off-platform and skip the fee. The defenses are practical, not legal: keep chat + screenshots + history in-app, don't surface contact info, make in-app purchase the only way to pay, and let reputation (reviews, badges) be something a coach loses by leaving. Convenience is the moat — the moment off-platform is easier, we've lost.

07

Trust & the ghostwriting line

This category has one genuine landmine, and we should decide where we stand before writing code, because it shapes copy, moderation, and terms.

The line we draw

There's a difference between coaching ("here's a better opener, here's why your bio isn't landing") and impersonation ("log into my account and message my matches as me"). The impersonation model is where catfishing complaints, platform bans, and PR risk live.

MVP stance: we're a coaching product. Coaches advise and draft suggestions the client sends themselves — they never operate the client's dating account. Same revenue, far less liability. We can revisit done-for-you later, deliberately, with real guardrails.

Beyond that, standard marketplace trust hygiene:

  • Reviews & ratings — the primary quality signal; a coach's aggregate rating and count drive ranking.
  • Response-time badge — sets client expectations and rewards active coaches.
  • Escrow on every purchase — the client's dollars are captured at purchase but only released to the coach on delivery/approval; enables clean refunds for disputes.
  • Report / block — table-stakes safety on any chat product.
08

Architecture & what we reuse

Honest framing: no single open-source repo is this product, because the novel part — metering dollars against engagements on each coach delivery — is exactly what off-the-shelf chat and marketplace repos don't do. But the reuse is still heavy; it's just at the component level.

NeedWhat we useWhy
App frameworkNext.js + TSFull-stack in one repo; API routes for the money paths
Auth · DB · storage · realtimeSupabaseOne dependency: users, Postgres + RLS, screenshot uploads, live chat transport. OSS & self-hostable
Payments & payoutsStripe ConnectMarketplace split, coach KYC, scheduled payouts — nothing OSS competes here
UI componentsshadcn/ui + TailwindFast, clean, un-templated primitives we own the code for
Forms & validationreact-hook-form + zodOne schema validates client, server, and DB edges
Marketplace patternsSharetribe templateRead it for listings/search/review ideas — don't adopt wholesale (hosted paid backend, non-metered chat)
The one rule that keeps money correct

Every money-moving action — purchase, delivery consumption, escrow release, payout, refund — runs server-side in a single database transaction that writes the ledger. The client UI never mutates money state directly. Get this boundary right and the rest of the app can move fast without risking someone's money.

10

Open questions

  • Where does the code live? A fresh dedicated repo is the clean answer — this product is unrelated to capbit's Rust auth library. (Or, if you want to dogfood capbit as the permissions layer, that's a deliberate and interesting choice we'd make on purpose.)
  • Cold-start: which niche do we seed first? Narrower is better — e.g. "men, Hinge, opener + conversation help" — so early supply and demand actually meet.
  • Pricing: confirm the 20% take rate, and the micro-charge batching window for per-reply items (daily feels right).
  • Coach vetting: open sign-up vs. light application for the first cohort (I lean light application to protect early quality).

Recommended next step: confirm this direction, then I scaffold Milestone 0 in a fresh repo — Next.js + Supabase + shadcn, with the profile and browse screens on seeded data — so you have something clickable within the first pass.
